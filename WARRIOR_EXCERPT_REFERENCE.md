# WARRIOR — Excerpt Reference (Outline, AI Info, Isaac & Ezra Dossiers)

## PART 1: STORY OUTLINE (CURRENT)

### World Foundation
The story starts in 1950. World War II ran five years longer, ending under nuclear fire: Moscow occupied, Berlin under atomic hellfire, Japan heavily depopulated. No lasting world peace, only balkanized fiefdoms.

In the early '70s, pan-Hellenism revives. Aetheas Stronos becomes president of the Hellenic Federation (southern Balkans to the Aegean), dreams of a new Rome, plans to invade Lebanon, Syria, Turkey. He has a Soviet nuclear program.

### Three Acts Structure

**Act I**: Humanity's devolution into warring states. Aetheas, with King Carlos Hugo of Spain, plans conquest. The Hellenic invasion of Lebanon begins with amphibious assault at Beirut, massive casualties. Gets stuck in the mud—the USA arms every warfighter. Ten years keeping insurgency under control, plans fall apart.

**Aage Stronos**: Born when his mother dies giving birth to him. Aetheas names him Aage. Aage is raised timid, kept small by his father's negligence and abuse. Made to watch Aetheas's war, expected to carry on the torch.

**Justine**: Princess of Spain, betrothed to Aage as infant. As they grow, she sees the madness and despairs; Aage is expected to keep the torch burning.

**Marcus**: Aetheas's advisor, essentially Aage's uncle. A voice of conscience, appalled but silent.

**Aaron Considine**: Aage's childhood friend (note: add continuity for Act II).

**The A'Had Family**: Prequel chapters show victims of Aetheas's indifference. Later in Act II, they plant wheat against nuclear winter, almost starve, survive. A firebreak blessed by A'Had's wife saves the crop from wildfire—metaphor for God's protection. Story goes dark until Act III.

**The G-Man**: Visits Aetheas, warns of unforeseen consequences. Words haunt him forever.

**Key Events**: Aage and Justine come to odds. Aage is abducted/captured (details evolve). Torture and captivity. Explosion. Aetheas learns of his son's fate. Act I ends with Aage's emergence into the alien occupation.

### The World and Story
**Broken Century**: Alternate WWII, five years longer, ends under nuclear fire. World balkanizes into fiefdoms.

**Hellenic Federation**: 1970s pan-Hellenism. Aetheas Stronos president, dreams new Rome, invades Lebanon.

**The Combine**: Alien empire (uses Half-Life vocabulary: Breen, City 17, Civil Protection, Overwatch, headcrabs, zombies, Striders, Vortigaunts, G-Man). Arrives after humanity's own cruelty. Seven Hour War kills ~90% of humanity. Suppression field stops reproduction ~10 years.

**Hidden Metaphysics (LOCKED, never stated)**: God of Abraham is real. Seven Hour War was secretly the rapture. Combine are legions of hell. G-Man is Lucifer. Com is Jesus. Book reads nihilistic until Act III.

### Premise (One Paragraph)
Warrior is three-act novel about Aage Stronos: timid son of would-be emperor, destroyed by war and torture, rebuilt by alien occupation into terrifying weapon (the Warrior), finally made human by fatherhood—specifically newborn Ezra and feral boy Isaac in small farming community after world's end. Governing theme: violence begets violence until someone chooses to be the last one (Romans 12:21: "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good"). Aage is cycle of violence made flesh; sanctification is moment he refuses to pass it on. NOT retroactive absolution.

### Birth Story (The Seven Hour War)
His parents shelter in apartment bathroom (no windows). Building comes down. Father throws himself over mother and child; says "I love you." Mother curls around him; says "My baby." Last word: "Live."

Isaac survives in pocket their bodies make, cries from hunger and cold. Crying saves him—firefighter stops, hears it, digs for hours by hand. Mother's stiffened hand moved to lift him out.

Wrapped in dead firefighter's coat with "Isaac" stitched on breast. Card tied to coat: "male infant, approximately newborn, parents deceased, name Isaac." Ink runs after six days; coat taken for colder child, but name already passed mouth to mouth.

### The Caregiver House
Official name: Municipal Children's Relief Annex. Everyone calls it "the house."

**The Woman**: Old, swollen-ankled, gray hair pinned with black clip. Smallest children call her mama; she flinches when they do. To Isaac, she is "God." Had children and grandchildren who died. Keeps one red mitten in top drawer, rubs thumb when thinks no one watching. Counts children morning and night.

**Young Isaac**: Likes being held, place by stove, blue chipped cup, wooden horse with one missing leg. Hides under table in thunder, wets himself at gunfire. Grabs woman's wrist when she tries to leave (when he has flu with Sera), and she stays awhile.

### The Combine Arrival & Escape
Masked soldiers with vocoders turn speech to noise. Tall machine enters. Woman hits first soldier with stove iron; shot twice. Isaac flees through window (falls, injures ankle, burns wrist on pipe). Finds dead firefighter in the building, wraps in his coat ("Isaac" stitched inside). Scavenges food for weeks in building wreckage.

Then the road: moves at night, sleeps in barns, steals. Develops hypervigilance. Burns become pale puckered scars. Hands scarred from glass and survival.

### City 20 & the Road
Age 8, City 20 uprising. Isaac already feral. Steals, fights, survives alone. Witnesses violence, learns how to disappear.

On the road, age 9-10: moves alone through occupied territory. Thin, hunting rats and scraps. White-knuckled grip on survival. No softness left, only calculation.

### Meeting Aage
First meeting age ~10-11. Aage in the Orchards already. Isaac hostile, deeply untrusting. Sees Aage as threat or tool. Doesn't believe care is real.

### Key Behaviors & Traits
- **Hypervigilance**: Watches, listens, never fully sleeps. Rubs burn scar on wrist to stay awake.
- **Survival instinct**: Everything filtered through "how do I not die"
- **Belligerence**: Defensive, sarcastic, angry. Expects betrayal.
- **Depth under anger**: When softness allowed, capable of devotion. Inexplicably melts with Matt (same age). With Aage, gradually learns safety real.
- **Hunger**: Physical and emotional. Steals bread, hoards food early on. Later, hungry for love he doesn't know how to receive.
- **Transformation**: From feral animal to boy to young man. Process slow, nonlinear, full of setbacks.

### Relationships
- **Aage**: Initially hostile, gradually becomes his entire world. Calls him Dad. Fierce protectiveness.
- **Ezra**: 10 years younger, brother relationship. Argues with him, teases him, protects him.
- **Matt**: Born same day/war as Isaac. Inexplicably melts; become great friends against logic.
- **Elena**: "Screamed about a girl named Elena" during fever (Aage's memory). Implied past trauma or significant relationship.

### Permanent Marks
- Burn scar on wrist (digs nails into it to stay awake)
- White scars on both palms
- Psychological: PTSD from Seven Hour War, caregiver house, road, and everything after

### Adult Isaac (The Things We Carry, ~25)
Broad through shoulders, dark hair with first gray, thick wool coat patched at elbows, hunting knife on belt. Short beard, thinner sharper face. Same eyes—older, kinder (though he'd refuse the word). Moves with authority. Has learned to love and be loved.

## PART 4: EZRA — COMPREHENSIVE CHARACTER DOSSIER

### Personality (LOCKED RULES)
- **Never negative**: Never says anything nasty, never cynical. Never "matured" through cruelty.
- **"Gooey"**: Affectionate, bright, full of life. Smile reaches people at restoration level.
- **Inclusive**: Includes everyone—birds, squirrels, mice, other children, adults.
- **Fascinated by**: Christmas lights, animals, ornaments, people, Miri, anything beautiful.
- **Afraid of**: Storms. In Thunderstorm scene, bursts into Aage's tent soaked and screaming, climbs like monkey, cries all night.
- **Mischief**: Steals bread, denies it with crumbs on face (Stay With Me). Brings Isaac bread with honey when sick. Teases and argues with Isaac.
- **Competitive**: Has fox's hunting instincts with sweets (Epilogue).
- **Arc philosophy**: Ezra's story radiates outward. His existence changes others. His arc is not about changing—he doesn't mature into cynicism. Files confirm he stays essentially himself.

### Early Life: The Embryo Stage (First ~6 Months at Orchards)
**Setting**: Pitch-black room smelling of sweat, old blankets, warm milk, baby breath. Aage and Ezra tangled under blankets, look like one organism from doorway.

**Aage's process**: Body detoxes from ten years war—sweats, shakes, vomits, sleeps hours. Ezra is catalyst and fuel. Holding him and waking when he cries gives damaged nervous system new discharge.

**Not full healing**: Acute poison leaves first. Afterward Aage feels hollowed out, "like building after fire." Ezra is "only thing growing in there."

**Body recognition**: Aage's body stops recognizing where he ends, Ezra begins. If someone carries Ezra across room, Aage experiences distance physically. When returned, his body settles immediately.

**Community context**: A'Had agreed to Aage's stay only because Com asked. A'Had's rule: everyone works. Com's answer: "By allowing him to sleep."

### Aage & Ezra Bond
**Primordial relationship**: Exists below reason, at mammalian level. Aage and Isaac relate through mind, identity, trauma, instruction. Aage and Ezra relate through body, warmth, smell, touch, dependence, possession, proximity, feeding, sleeping, carrying.

**Not especially masculine/feminine**: Aage's ordinary social categories burned out. Becomes Ezra's entire parent: father, mother, shelter, warmth, food, weapon. Can be intensely tender and intensely dangerous without seeing contradiction.

**Animal resonance**: Aage stares at Ezra for hours without checking for danger or thinking sentimental thoughts. Bodies settle into each other. Ezra's breathing, heat, smell, tiny movements, weight become part of Aage's internal environment. Aage's preferred configuration: Ezra pressed against him.

**Possessiveness**: Not "my precious" ownership but physical lack of boundary. Aage's body almost fails to recognize separation. Distance hurts him physically.

## PART 5: KEY SCENES FOR REFERENCE

### Act III — The Sparring (Isaac 24, Aage nearly 50)
Aage and Isaac spar at dawn. Aage raises hand slow, bionic hand open, light catching metal. Isaac meets it. Wordless choreography of their entire relationship. Isaac learned watching Aage for 11 years. Fight becomes meditation, timeless. Isaac mirrors Aage's opening—slow, high, open. Aage stops. Isaac says, "Where did you—?" / "You think I wasn't watching? Every day. Eleven years." Isaac brings hand down. Aage lets him have it. Hits the dust on his back. Isaac reaches down. Aage takes his hand. "You let me." / "I didn't." / "I know. That's what I mean."

### Act III — The Crowbar (Isaac 13)
Isaac finds crowbar in shed, carries it like rifle. Hits barrel with it. CLANG. Aage's face goes white. Falls. Convulses. Isaac panics. "HELP!" Aage lies still, eye open, not blinking. Isaac thinks he's dead. "Come on, wake up. Please." Convulsions stop. Aage goes rigid. Isaac shakes him. "Wake up!" Eye doesn't blink. Isaac backs away then lunges forward. "COM!" His voice tears. "COM! COM!" Nobody comes. Convulsions stop. Aage's eye open, fixed on sky. Doesn't blink. Isaac doesn't know what to do. He's 13. Knows how to survive. Doesn't know how to save his father.

### Act III — Isaac's Question (by well)
Isaac asks why clouds different colors, fast like he's regretting it. "I mean, you don't have to answer. It's dumb." / "It's not dumb." / "I should know that already. I'm thirteen." Aage explains: water drops, light scatters blue, you see white; thick clouds, light can't get through, you see gray; sun behind them, orange or red. Isaac listens, really listening. "So it's like... they're not actually different colors. It's just... the light." / "Yeah." / "That's kind of beautiful." Says it quietly, surprised at own thought, risk he's only now willing to take. They sit. "Do you think they look like that in winter?" / "Maybe. But same reason." / "Oh." He swings feet. "I was wondering if maybe the cold makes them look sad." Aage: "Maybe. Maybe that's part of it." Isaac nods, satisfied. "Can I ask another one?" / "You can ask as many as you want." Isaac thinks like offered something fragile. "Why does Ezra laugh at nothing?" / "He's okay. He's just happy. Kids do that." / "I don't remember doing that." Aage pulls him against his side. Isaac goes stiff, then lets himself rest there. "You're doing it now. Just slower. And with more questions." Isaac doesn't answer but doesn't pull away. After a while his head drops to Aage's shoulder, eyes close against bright afternoon. Just a kid by a well with someone who loved him.

### Act III — Isaac's Silly Potato Game
Isaac stands in common room with potato on his head. "I'm a potato. I'm the potato king." Potato falls off. "It betrayed me." He holds it up. "This potato has no respect." Bonks himself with it. "You have to listen. You're food. That's your job." Ages voice (squeaky): "Potato! Get your potato! This potato is—round!" Aage laughs—real one, surprised. Isaac lights up. Does it again, voice even more stupid. "Very round potato! Best potato! One time this potato—" He looks at potato like it might help. "—was a potato!" Aage laughing harder. Isaac goes off rails, whole thing with potato talking in squeaky voice. "Potato, why are you round?" / "Because I am potato." / "That makes sense." / "Yes. I am very smart potato." Isaac laughing at own stupidity. Bonks potato against his own head. "This potato is dumb. Worst potato." Throws it gently on Aage's lap. "Potato is your problem now. Potato is mean." Aage bent over, actually laughing. Isaac escalates—whole war between two potatoes yelling at each other in terrible squeaky voice. "You are bad potato!" / "No, YOU are bad potato!" / "I'm telling mom!" Potato freezes. Makes face. "Potato doesn't have a mom. Potato just exists." Sets potatoes down, makes them hug, kissing sounds. "Now you're friends." Aage laughing so hard he's shaking. Isaac sits, vibrating with joy at making Aage laugh like that, at being this stupid and ridiculous and it's okay, nobody hitting him for wasting time. Flops into seat next to Aage, completely satisfied, grinning so hard cheeks red. "Was that funny?" suddenly uncertain. "You were very funny." / "The potato was funny?" / "The potatoes were hilarious." Isaac grins, leans against Aage's shoulder, picks potato back up, holds it against chest like precious. "Did you hear that? We did good." Aage puts arm around kid and ridiculous potatoes, lets himself exist in moment where world is light and stupid and full of child's joy.

### Act III — The Fire Goes Out (Aage and Ezra)
Aage awake while Ezra sleeps against his side. Back begins to burn. Opens eyes, tries—mistake. Room disappears. Heat spreads. Line beneath skin. Little spark under scars. Then another. Breathing stops. Opens eyes again. Dark room. Knows where he is. Back burns anyway. Pulls shoulder. Pain answers. No injury. Scars old. Body doesn't care. Fire spreads. Feels iron touch him. Hand clenches blanket. Looks at shutters. Three lines of moonlight. Orchard. No concrete. No drain. No door with footsteps. No bottle rolling. Smell comes anyway. Burned cloth. Sweat. Something rotten. Swallows. Mouth dry. Room too small. Blankets too heavy. Ezra too close. Everything too close. Pulls arm out, presses palm against mattress. Wood frame. Wool. Clean cloth. Knows these things. Body doesn't. Burning climbs into shoulders. Heart begins to pound. Not fast—hard. Each beat strikes ribs from inside. Breathes in. Can't finish it. Tries again. Half breath. Chest refuses rest. Sits up. Slowly. Ezra shifts but doesn't wake. Cold air touches face. Heat on back becomes worse. Reaches behind. Finds ridges. Old scars. Thick places. Smooth places. Places where skin never learned to be skin again. Touches one. Half second feels someone else's hand there. Rips hand away. Heart accelerates. Room narrows. Moonlight too bright. Hears breathing. Not Ezra's. His. Too fast. Tries to stop. Makes it worse. Something coming. Doesn't know what. That's oldest part. Door will open. Hand will grab. Light will come on. Bad man will walk through. Body prepares before mind says bad man gone. Shoulders lock. Stomach tightens. Jaw hurts. Sweat beneath hair. Back on fire. Leans forward, both feet on floor. Cold boards. Real. Presses down with toes. Wood. Knows wood. Room moves anyway. Vision tunnels. Bed. Wall. Shutters. Concrete. No. Wood. Concrete. Wood. Someone laughing. Nothing. Hands shake. Stares. One flesh. One not. That helped moment. Other hand hadn't existed then. Enough? Wasn't. Stands. Knee nearly gives. Catches side of bed. Ezra stirs behind him. Freezes. One terrible second thinks someone else moved. Then he makes sound. Small. Half asleep. Not word. Just tired noise in back of throat. Turns. Ezra rolled toward warm place left behind. Hand moved across sheet. Searching. Still asleep. Fingers found nothing. Face tightened. Made noise again. Everything stopped. Not panic—kept happening. Heart hammering. Back burning. Room shrinking. But something else entered. Ezra. Watched his hand move over empty sheet. Asleep. Didn't know what doing. Years ago, when barely bigger than Aage's forearm, done exactly same thing. Wake without waking. Reach. Find him. Settle. Chest hurt. [Continues—this scene is long and profound; ends with Aage unable to leave the bed because Ezra needs him there.]

## PART 6: THEMES & GOVERNING PRINCIPLES

**Governing Theme**: Violence begets violence until someone chooses to be the last one. (Romans 12:21: "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good.")

**Aage's Arc**: From cycle of violence made flesh to sanctification—moment he refuses to pass it on. NOT retroactive absolution.

**Redemption**: Not through violent victory but through care, presence, love. Only attachment heals.

**The Three Characters**:
- **Aage**: Warrior broken down to rebuild around life instead of death. Learning to love.
- **Isaac**: Feral survival becoming human connection. Learning to be held.
- **Ezra**: First new life after apocalypse. Radiates joy and restoration outward.

**Hidden Metaphysics** (never stated, implied at end): God real. Seven Hour War was rapture. Combine legions of hell. G-Man is Lucifer. Com is Jesus. Book reads nihilistic until Act III reveals all-along truth.

**Sacrifice and Presence**: "Nobody becomes himself alone." / "We live until we have carried them as far as we can." The difference between remaining alive and living is what you carry.
