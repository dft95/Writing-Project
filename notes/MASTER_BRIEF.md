## **WARRIOR — Master Story & Character Brief**

**Focus: Aage, Isaac and Ezra.** Compiled November 1, 2026 from the author's Warrior Drive: the Codex 00–12, the character dossiers, the story bibles and the draft scenes. This one document is meant to give another AI (ChatGPT) everything it needs to understand Warrior and write new chapters in its voice.

## **PART 0 — HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT (instructions for the AI)**

1.  Read the whole document before writing anything. Section A (at the top) is the content boundaries. Part 1 is the rules. Part 2 is the world. Parts 3–5 are the three main characters. Part 6 is the supporting cast. Part 7 is style and voice. Part 8 tells you how to write a chapter. Part 9 lists open questions you must NOT settle on your own.
2.  **Status words.** LOCKED = settled canon; never contradict it. CURRENT = the preferred direction; use it. WORKING = provisional; usable, but the author may change it. DRAFT = from a prose draft that hasn't been promoted to canon. OUTLINE = from an older master outline; it loses to anything newer. OPEN = undecided; ask the author or write around it.
3.  When this document gives a specific detail (an age, a line, an object), keep it exact. Don't invent new hard facts (names of dead family members, birthplaces, dates) unless the author asks you to. Invent texture freely: weather, food, chores, minor villagers, small dialogue.
4.  When you write a chapter, say in one line at the top where it sits in the timeline and how old Aage, Isaac and Ezra are in it.

## **PART 1 — HARD RULES (LOCKED, apply to every chapter)**

1.  **Matt and his parents always live.** Never hurt them, never kill them, never raise the idea.
2.  **Com's identity is never stated outright.** He is secretly Jesus. Write him so that a re-reader recognizes it, while a first-time reader sees only an unusually grounded, patient doctor-priest.
3.  **Ezra is never made cynical or mean to "mature" him.** He never says anything nasty.
4.  **Vengeance never heals Aage.** Jibril's death, Calveigh's death and Aetheas's death each feed the monster or change nothing. Only attachment heals him.
5.  **Aage is not invincible.** He bleeds, breaks, seizes, loses limbs and nearly dies. His edge is willingness to pay costs other people can't, not immunity to cost.
6.  **Don't explain subtext.** Don't announce themes. If told not to do something, simply leave it out; don't write a sentence pointing out that it didn't happen.

## **PART 2 — THE WORLD AND THE STORY**

### **2.1 Premise in one paragraph**

Warrior is a three-act, long-form novel about Aage Stronos: a timid son of a would-be emperor who is destroyed by war and torture, rebuilt by an alien occupation into a terrifying weapon called the Warrior, and finally made human again by fatherhood, specifically by a newborn named Ezra and a feral boy named Isaac, in a small farming community after the end of the world. Its governing theme is that violence begets violence until someone chooses to be the last one (Romans 12:21: "Be not overcome of evil, but overcome evil with good"). Aage is the cycle of violence made flesh, and his sanctification is the moment he refuses to pass it on. That sanctification is not retroactive absolution.

### **2.2 The world**

  - **The Broken Century.** An alternate history. World War II runs about five years longer and ends under nuclear fire: Moscow occupied, a Berlin and alpine-redoubt endgame, Japan heavily depopulated. No lasting peace, no "never again," no world police; the world balkanizes into fiefdoms (OUTLINE, CURRENT broadly).
  - **The Hellenic Federation / Aethos.** In the early 1970s pan-Hellenism revives. Aetheas Stronos becomes president of a federation covering roughly the southern Balkans to the Aegean, dreams of a new Rome, and invades Lebanon, aiming next at Syria and Turkey. "Aethos" sometimes names his political order.
  - **The Combine.** An alien empire (the corpus uses Half-Life vocabulary: Combine, Dr. Wallace Breen, City 17, Civil Protection, Overwatch, headcrabs, zombies, Striders, Vortigaunts, the G-Man). It arrives after humanity has already built its own machinery of cruelty, so human evil is not the aliens' fault. The **Seven Hour War** wipes out about 90% of humanity. A **suppression field** stops human reproduction for about ten years. Occupied humanity is categorized, relocated, policed, experimented on and drugged into compliance.
  - **Hidden metaphysics** (LOCKED, never stated outright, implied most strongly at the end): the God of Abraham is real. The Seven Hour War was secretly the rapture. The Combine are secretly the legions of hell. The G-Man is Lucifer. Com is Jesus. The book reads as nihilistic until the last act.
  - **The Orchards / Tarsus.** After the suppression field falls, Act III takes place in a small community of stone houses, terraces, olive and fig trees, horses and fields: the settlement of the A'Had family, known as the Orchards, in a sparse network of settlements in Anatolia/the Aegean region. In the finale every settlement gathers at Tarsus. (Exact geography is OPEN: Aegean coast vs southeastern Anatolia.)

### **2.3 Act I — The Broken Century, Lebanon, Justine, Captivity (planning chapters 1–300)**

  - **Ch1–50**: the rise of Aethos. Aage's mother Europa dies giving birth to him (Ch3); asked to choose, Aetheas says "Save the child," and her death is scrubbed from the records. Aetheas: "Humanity does not require freedom. It requires structure." (Ch4.) Young Aage, asked what holds the council together, answers "Fear," and his father approves (Ch5). Aage grows up timid and quiet beside Justine (a Spanish princess he was betrothed to in infancy) and his friend Aaron Considine. Marcus, his father's comrade, is a moral uncle. The Hellenic invasion of Beirut includes a missile strike on an apartment tower that kills the wife and two daughters of a man named Jibril.
  - **Ch51–150**: the occupation becomes a quagmire. The A'Had family suffers under it (a civilian thread). A mysterious "man in the suit" (the G-Man) warns Aetheas of "unforeseen consequences." Jibril's grief hardens into hatred.
  - **Ch151–199**: the ninth year. Justine wants to escape the dynastic cage. On a snowy night (Ch195) she says "Come with me." Before he can answer, a car bomb (Ch199) kills her and takes Aage's right leg below the knee. The bomb was ordered by John Calveigh, a gunrunner who chose the son as more useful leverage than the father.
  - **Ch200–250**: captivity. Aage, aged 20, is held about three months in a Beirut basement by Jibril and two men, Abbas and Umar. He is starved, beaten, burned, branded with a star and crescent over his left breast, struck in the skull with a crowbar, and finally suffers cardiac arrest. In the black void he meets Justine and strangles the timid child he used to be.
  - **Ch251–300**: what came back. Aage returns brain-damaged and fearless in the worst way. He fakes a seizure and kills Abbas and Umar with his bare hands, but is caught on the stairs (Ch255). Calveigh confesses to ordering the bomb and has him returned to his father's people (the exact Ch255 events are OPEN). Coma, catatonia, a year or two of rehabilitation. Jibril is captured and Aetheas lets Aage torture and kill him (Ch276–277). Marcus confronts Aetheas, reveals that Europa never wanted Aage sacrificed to his ambition, and Aetheas shoots him (Ch287–290). Marcus's death is the first thing to break through Aage's numbness. Calveigh escapes with Delta Force's help (Ch286). As the Seven Hour War begins, Aage shoots his father twice (Ch298); Aetheas survives, paralyzed from the waist down. Act I ends with Aage running alone into the ruins.

### **2.4 Act II — Firebase Lancaster and City 17**

  - **Lancaster** (four to five years, near Lake Ladoga and St. Petersburg): Aage is routed north by trains and radio. Firebase Lancaster, under Captain Judge (Combine armor plus a western trench coat), feeds and protects survivors while categorizing and experimenting on them. Aage reunites with Aaron. Everyone is dosed with Dr. Breen's Generation I compliance drug. In the drug void Breen reaches out a hand and repeats Justine's words, "Come with me," and Aage accepts him. Aage becomes the program's breakthrough subject, gets a bionic right leg, and splits into a wounded animal and an obedient soldier. He learns to fight and learns to be mean. Against the Russian "Motherland Regiment" he wins a tactically brilliant, morally rotten battle (a girl named Nina survives to become the fairy-tale "Mother Russia"). The **Fratricide**: during a refugee crisis, fuel shortage and flood, Aaron mutinies and opens the gate. The OTA massacre the crowd, and Aage, in his soldier persona, orders his comrades to kill each other, with the survivor promised life. Aaron is the survivor, and Aage sends him to Nova Prospekt and believes him dead. Aage is demoted.
  - **City 17**: Aage becomes Civil Protection unit CP-698 (designation MTF.1.17.4.698), masked and numbered. His superior is the district commander CP-628. Seizures and decline follow. His eyes are destroyed and replaced with glowing blue bionics (around Ch74–77), and his right arm is crushed and replaced with a bionic arm (around Ch146–148). Combine conditioning becomes counterfeit revelation: orders feel like a column of fire telling him what his suffering was for. He can't tell whether he is "the sword of heaven or the dog of hell." Breen marks him as a Chosen Vessel, the intended future host for Lucifer. The resistance's "Madman" is Calveigh (Ch198). Calveigh tries to execute him in an old butcher shop, but the gun clicks (Ch204). Aage detaches his bionic hand to slip the cuffs and stabs him (Ch206). Dying, Calveigh says he's Polish, from the nuclear exclusion zone, and that the man in the suit visited him too. This triggers the **Lion vision** (Ch207): the strangled child returns, scarred and bloodied, and asks not to be forgotten, and Aage promises. With Eli's cafeteria speech, Aage ignites the uprising (Ch208–213). He kills the Sector Commander (Ch223–227) and CP-628 in a knife duel on 628's falling command platform. At the villa he finishes his crippled, cybernetic father. In the burning family home, stirred by his mother's spirit, he goes up the stairs for the worn white lion from his childhood bed. He leaves the ruined city (Ch236) and begins the Pilgrimage south toward the sea.

### **2.5 Act III — The Return of Life**

  - Aage finds a dying woman on a hilltop with a newborn. She whispers the name Ezra, and Aage promises "I will keep him safe." He carries the baby down to the A'Had family's settlement, the Orchards.
  - The **Embryo Stage** (about six months): Aage detoxes from ten years of war wrapped around the infant in a dark room, while the community, persuaded by the priest-doctor Com, protects the cocoon.
  - **Isaac**, a feral ten-year-old already living wild at the Orchards, meets him: first the wolf night, then the bread fight. A long war turns into the snowball fight, then silent attachment, then the Depot, where Isaac is gutted by a zombie and says "Dad?", then surgery, fever and Isaac's whole backstory, and then the years of father and son.
  - The **beauty years**: hunts, patrols, wells, horses, mercy, ruins, the Road Wardens, the return to City 20, courtship, a wedding.
  - The **finale**: the Combine return. Com, Miriam and two of A'Had's three sons die, and the orchard burns. Aage gives the elegy. At the last battle he sends Isaac for help, brings down a Strider, and loses his bionic right arm. The Combine Avatar unmasks as Aaron, now Lucifer's host. Isaac's cavalry charge spears Aaron, and Aaron's blood reaches Isaac. The Vortigaunts and finally God (the Great Beyond) stop Lucifer from taking Isaac. Aage dies. Justine comes out of the light: "Come with me." He goes. He is buried beside her grave. Epilogue: rebuilding, Isaac's wife wanting children, the big blue sky.
  - **The end of humanity (LOCKED, author-stated):** After the story is over, humanity does not have enough people to replace itself. Over several generations it goes extinct and all life on Earth is gone. It is a good ending: humanity itself has been redeemed and the original sin has been repaid.
  - **Epilogue to the epilogue (WORKING, author unsure):** After every earthly body is gone, God's kingdom comes back to earth. The whole story is about the rapture and humanity's redemption: all those who are bad are burned away, maybe only the good are left, and then they are gone too. The story of humanity is concluded.

## **PART 3 — AAGE STRONOS**

### **3.1 Central conception (LOCKED)**

Aage's arc is not weakness to strength. It is the destruction of one human configuration, the emergence of a terrifying survival configuration, and the extraordinarily hard recovery of enough humanity that strength no longer has to mean cruelty. The shape: a frightened child is shattered; a revenant grows in the wreckage; the revenant becomes the Warrior; the Warrior becomes a father; and the father becomes human enough to sacrifice himself without hatred giving the sacrifice its meaning. The Warrior is frightening because the reader remembers the boy who stood there before him.

### **3.2 Vital facts**

  - Full name Aage Stronos (rare variant spellings: Aagi, Ayagi). Born in the early 1970s of the story's timeline. 20 at the abduction. Mid-40s at his death.
  - Eyes green before the captivity. In the last basement days the visible eye looks almost black. After recovery: pale skin, pale gray hair, a distant, altered gaze. From City 17 on: glowing blue bionic eyes.
  - Body as archive: right leg lost below the knee in the car bomb, later a bionic right leg. A crushed orbit and depressed skull fracture from the crowbar. Clothing-iron burns across his back. Jibril's star-and-crescent brand over his left breast (a mark of domination, not a judgment on the symbol). A bionic right arm from City 17, with a detachable hand and a glove over it in Act III. His left hand is flesh, "the one that could still feel the air." In the finale the Strider blast takes the bionic right arm at the shoulder.
  - Horse: Justine, a black mare named after her. Gear in Act III: a .357 revolver, a duster coat (OUTLINE), an M14 in the finale.
  - His real baseline, his domestic no-mask self, is a t-shirt and boxers (author note). Seeing him torn out of that baseline is part of why the captivity scenes land.
  - Not a believer through nearly the whole book (LOCKED). "God, please save me" under torture is the last appeal of a frightened person, not faith. He wants truth more than consolation.

### **3.3 Childhood and Justine**

  - His existence begins with a death and with his father choosing succession over his wife. He grows up materially insulated and emotionally exposed to power, hearing adults talk about people as populations, assets and leverage.
  - His survival strategy is compliance: soft-spoken, hesitant, conflict-avoidant. Not stupid; perceptive enough to make himself small around danger.
  - Justine is his first human world. Draft scenes: the winter breakwater under one coat ("If we had a daughter… I'd want her to be loud." His wish: "I'd want her to not be afraid of the house… of her father."); the roof of the east wing (life is "a lend"; "You're the warmest person I've ever met. You hide it like contraband."; "If it's ever a choice between being safe and being alive, pick alive."); the stables, where she describes her grandmother's house south of Valencia with a door that locks from the inside ("Not knowing is allowed. Not answering isn't."); and the steps in the snow ("Come with me… Tell me in the car. You can have until the gate."). He counts every kiss. He has his mouth open to say yes when the street turns white.
  - Prologue concept: in a summer field Justine reads Romans 12:21 and asks how the cycle of hurt ever stops. "Someone has to be the last one." She tucks a white flower behind his ear: "You look beautiful."
  - Verbal tic carried through life: "I know." Crisis lines: "I'm here." / "I know."

### **3.4 The captivity: destruction, not training (LOCKED)**

  - The captivity doesn't harden him. It damages him, and any later strength keeps the shape of the wound.
  - The reader should feel the hostage contract break: at first they assume Aage survives because he's valuable; then they realize Jibril means to kill him slowly.
  - Escalation: whippings, mock executions, a sham Russian roulette. A forced "choice" between his life and an old prisoner's: Aage says he wants to live and Jibril kills the old man ("You chose yourself" is the lie). Jibril punches him hard enough to fracture the orbit, and Abbas and Umar have to drag him off. Forced feeding with a funnel and scalding soup. Then the crowbar, about a month before release: blood fans across the wall and Abbas and Umar think he's dead. Jibril: "Get the water. He's not finished." The crowbar is Jibril's one total loss of control; afterward he's briefly gentle, protecting his toy, then turns to burns and the brand.
  - Late captivity, Aage's world divides into "awake time" and "bad time"; Jibril is "the bad man."
  - The reaching hand: days after the cardiac arrest, while being beaten, Aage weakly reaches toward the door. It's the first coherent act, it accomplishes nothing, and it later proves that purpose was reassembling. (Damaged-brain image: cartoon yellow birds.)
  - Cardiac arrest: several minutes clinically dead. In the void he meets Justine, who tells him in symbolic terms that he must burn away fear to survive. As the ring of fire closes in, he strangles the timid child. He ruptures back into consciousness and collapses into severe post-anoxic encephalopathy, not brain death.
  - Recovery curve: days 0–2 nearly absent; days 2–4 tracking and recoiling; days 4–6 guarding his body and watching hands and exits; days 6–8 a simple plan becomes possible. Purpose comes back before humanity.
  - The breakout (Ch255): twelve basement steps that for months meant terror coming down. He fakes a seizure, kills two captors with his bare hands, drags himself up the stairs and growls at Farid's rifle until the butt knocks him out. Inner experience: fragments, no names, no speeches. The last command is "live."
  - Calveigh's confession: "I ordered the bombing… The girl was an unfortunate complication… I killed her." Aage's face doesn't move. Calveigh watches grief replaced by a hatred so vast he imagines the devil spreading his wings, and he never turns his back on Aage again.

### **3.5 Neurology: the operating manual (LOCKED / CURRENT)**

  - **Cause**: cumulative injury, not a five-minute timer. About five minutes of oxygen deprivation, the crowbar skull fracture and crushed orbit, an untreated infected head wound, possible hematoma pressure, malnutrition and seizures.
  - **Fear**: threat recognition remains, but the conscious fear and inhibition layer is damaged. Old Aage: threat → dread → panic → begging or freezing. The Warrior: threat → recognition → action. That isn't bravery, because bravery requires fear. His fear alarm is broken.
  - **Mind and body uncoupled**: he can truthfully say he isn't afraid while his heart pounds, he sweats, freezes or seizes. The mind says nothing; the body is already preparing for war.
  - **Conditioned triggers**: Jibril's face, the crowbar's shape, posture, swing or ring, a phrase in Jibril's rhythm, a touch on the back of the neck. These can fire learned threat circuits without conscious fear. The body remembers in a language consciousness doesn't speak.
  - **Seizures**: post-traumatic epilepsy. Intermittent and consequential, never convenient or constant. Sometimes spontaneous; sometimes brought on by sleep loss, injury, stress or a sensory trigger. A severe panic attack can tip into a seizure.
  - **Panic attacks** about the torture last the rest of his life: nuclear at first, easing over the years, never gone.
  - **Fear returning is healing.** Young Aage had fear without action. The Warrior has action without fear. Mature Aage eventually has both, and that is actual courage.

### **3.6 The revenant in rehabilitation (WORKING scene bank)**

  - Medically induced coma, weeks of catatonia, one to two years of rehab. Marcus at his bedside; he stares at the big blue sky.
  - Language: Jibril is "the bad man," Justine is "her." Asked to describe her: "warm." Told she's gone: "know." He can write "Aage Stronos" but doesn't answer to it. Shown his old notebook, he asks where the young man who wrote it is.
  - Waits for instructions: blows out birthday candles when told to, then waits. Sits dressed on the bed edge for hours. Can't choose between two shirts. Imitates table manners one step behind everyone.
  - Threat behavior: watches doorways ("bad time," "who"), rearranges furniture to see the entrance, knows people by their footsteps, looks at a grabbing hand and then the throat, freezes completely at Calveigh's name, sleeps under the bed, hoards bread. After a triggered attack: "didn't mean." About a servant who made a mistake: "Who's going to hurt him?"
  - "The Burning": wakes believing the brand is being applied again, vomits, fights nurses, and says "still burning."
  - The white lion plush: the one object he insists on keeping in bed. He rejects a repaired "new face" version and hides it after Aetheas mocks him at dinner. It's an inherited attachment, not proof the child survived. The child stays dead until the Lion vision.
  - Aetheas mistakes usefulness for recovery. Marcus glimpses a familiar laugh, which is memory residue, not proof the boy lived.
  - Jibril's death: Aage's eyes light up for the first time, wrongly, with sadistic hunger, and he gives a low, terrible laugh. It is mostly kept offstage. He has become capable of the cruelty that destroyed him.
  - Marcus's death: Aage holds him, cries and begs. Proof that his humanity was inaccessible, not destroyed.

### **3.7 The Warrior in Act II**

  - Animal fighting: hands, teeth, knives, prosthetics, momentum, surrounding objects, all one offensive act. He closes distance others won't and trades pieces of himself for access. The old Aage made himself small so danger would pass him by; the Warrior becomes the thing danger withdraws from.
  - Tone: overwhelming epicness with a horror aftertaste. Every spectacular act should carry two truths: Aage is astonishing, and something is terribly wrong with Aage.
  - Cruelty without a sense of himself as evil: at his worst he sincerely believes he's the instrument of the good.
  - The meaning hunger: under everything is a young man asking the universe to make the pain mean something. Anything that offers a total explanation can reach him, and the Combine exploits exactly that.
  - The "biblical tear": even in the deepest City 17 conditioning he still dreams of Jibril. The basement predates the Combine, so they can never fully own him.

### **3.8 Aage in Act III: father**

  - **Arrival**: A'Had recognizes him as "the white devil" from the regime that killed his homeland and sends for a shotgun. Aage opens his coat to show the baby, says he killed his father, and is allowed to stay if he works (OUTLINE).
  - **Embryo Stage**: sweats, shakes, vomits, sleeps with Ezra on his chest. The body learns: Ezra cries and no one attacks; a door opens and no one tortures him; the child is still there when he wakes. Ezra doesn't cure him; he gives the nervous system a second language.
  - **The night before the bread fight** (LOCKED, secret): a few weeks in, a panic attack mixed with the brainwashing has him nearly strangle sleeping two-month-old Ezra. He makes no contact, pulls himself back to lucidity because of the child, walks out, and kills the wolf stalking Isaac. He tells no one, ever.
  - **With Isaac**: he can teach violence without cruelty, set boundaries without domination, protect without possessing, and be frightening without making Isaac afraid of him. That proves the chain can stop with him.
  - **Nadia** offers him love and a normal life on a Christmas Eve ride. He declines gently: too much has happened. "Maybe one day."
  - **Com**: friend and confessor. Years of meditation on the beach: rage, then self-hatred, then grief, then blind pain, then breakthrough. A campfire confession about Aaron and Lancaster (OUTLINE).
  - **Heroic beats** (DRAFT): a burning bunkhouse, through the wall metal arm first, carrying a child whose teeth are still in his wrist. A starving raider kid he spares, leaving bread while Isaac watches. A well he can't fit down, so he lowers Isaac, his hands shaking the whole time. A night he sits motionless in a dark doorway and lets something pass Ezra's house, the hardest fight of his life, which no one ever knows about. A burning barn, where he comes out with Hadi's mare.
  - **Voice**: blunt, observant, physical, first person when he's the POV. He can understand a feeling without naming it, with no self-therapy narration. His violence switches on abruptly, and the switch is what frightens.
  - **Three states**: the Child (fear, no action), the Revenant/Warrior (action, no fear), the Father (still dangerous, but fear, grief, humor and care coexist; the father contains the Warrior).

### **3.9 The finale and death**

  - **The Elegy** (after the first attack; the dead are laid by the roofless chapel). A'Had tries to speak, "Last night, we lost—", and can't. Aage stands. Com once told him "death was the last lesson we teach each other." Key lines: "There is nothing good about this… We should not make their deaths beautiful." / "Nobody becomes himself alone." / "The dead do not ask us to follow them into death. They ask us to continue what death interrupted." / "To plant again… Especially when we know it may burn again." / "We must become their hands… their hope… their courage." / "Let us leave them the part of us that refused to become what hurt us." / "We live until we have carried them as far as we can. And then someone else will carry us." He speaks about the dead, never explaining his own arc; the reader hears his life underneath. Closing thought: staying alive and living are not the same, and the difference is what you carry.
  - **The last battle** (DRAFT, first person): rearguard at a treeline with the old soldiers Farrow and Deniz while the caravan goes east to Tarsus. The Strider's footfalls. He sends Isaac for the army; Isaac turns in the saddle, "I love you, Dad\!" / "I love you too. Go\!" He makes himself watch Isaac ride away. To Farrow: "Best thing I ever did right. Didn't even know I was doing it at the time." Three claymore lines and a fifty-cal. The Strider's violet cannon erases his building. Under the rubble, voices (Justine, his mother, Com) say his name, "like being called home." "Not yet." He fights with a cracked M14, and for the first time the absence of fear is "just true," not a broken wire. He runs under the Strider and fires into the barrel seam, remembering everything he was. The blast takes his bionic right arm at the shoulder and leaves him unable to stand. Aaron, masked, comes to him. Aage apologizes: "I called you a traitor in front of everyone. You were right and I wouldn't listen." The avatar: "He can't hear you." Isaac's charge.
  - **Planned ending** (not yet drafted): Aaron returns for a moment on the spear. The blood reaches Isaac; the Vortigaunts hold Lucifer off; the G-Man claims Aage's soul and is refused. Aage to Isaac: "Stay with me for a minute. It's time." (Outline: Aage tells Isaac that he and Ezra saved him, and Isaac says he saved him too.) Justine: "Come with me." He says okay. A lion goes behind a hill and sleeps. Isaac and Ezra bury him beside Justine.

### **3.10 Final thesis**

Jibril teaches Aage's nervous system that another human being can become the entire universe of pain. Justine remains the buried memory that another human being can be good. Isaac teaches him that a frightened child doesn't need to be strangled to survive. Ezra teaches his body that closeness can continue without punishment. Strength doesn't require the destruction of fear; it requires becoming strong enough to carry fear without passing it on.

## **PART 4 — ISAAC**

### **4.1 Vital facts**

  - Name: Isaac. No surname. Named by the dead man's coat he was wrapped in after the rescue, with "Isaac" stitched on the breast. The narration doesn't call him Isaac before that.
  - Born at the start of the Seven Hour War. Exactly ten years older than Ezra (LOCKED). Matt is the same age.
  - **Age markers**: 4 days old when the building falls; 8 at the City 20 uprising; about 9 on the road and on reaching the Orchards; 10 at first meeting Aage (CURRENT); 11 at the snowball fight, Ezra 1 (LOCKED); 13 at the Depot, Ezra 3 (LOCKED); 14 in the post-Depot recovery scenes; 16 in the Brush Devil and Old Commander; 16–18 as a Road Warden; 18–20+ for the City 20 return and courtship; an adult (about 25 in The Things We Carry) at his marriage weeks before the finale.
  - **Look**: at 9, ribs showing, uneven dark hair, a chipped tooth, a barn coat too big for him. At 13: dark hair, freckles, brown eyes, pale and thin from fever after the Depot. A permanent burn scar on his wrist (he digs his nails into it to stay awake) and white scars across both palms. At 16: taller, broader, loud, with a double-barreled shotgun. As a man: broad shoulders, dark hair with the first gray, a patched wool coat, a hunting knife, a short beard, a thinner face, the same eyes, "older and kinder, though he would not thank you for saying so."
  - Horse: Aisha, a chestnut. Weapons over time: a stolen knife, a wooden practice knife, the shotgun, a rifle, and the cavalry spear in the finale.

### **4.2 Backstory, in order (CURRENT)**

1.  **Birth**: his parents shelter in a windowless apartment bathroom. The building falls. His father throws himself over them: "I love you." His mother curls around him: "My baby." Her last word: "Live." He survives in the pocket their bodies make. His hungry crying makes a passing firefighter stop. Five men from that station dig by hand for hours, and one has to move his mother's stiffened hand to lift him out.
2.  **The caregiver house** (Municipal Children's Relief Annex): an old, swollen-ankled woman, gray hair pinned with the same black clip, whom the little ones call "mama." She keeps one red mitten in a drawer, counts the children morning and night, apologizes when she thinks no one hears, and ties a ribbon on the wrist of any child who dies. Little Isaac likes the place by the stove, a blue chipped cup, and a wooden horse missing a leg. He hides under the table in thunder. The Combine come; she hits the first soldier with the stove iron and is shot twice. Her last silent words: "My babies." He learns that screaming saves no one, and by the second day on the train he makes no sounds at all.
3.  **City 20** (secretly Ankara, never stated): a train with no seats, Breen on the monitors: "Welcome home." A labor house in a converted school: stripping wire, windows painted white, a sealed basement door with a Combine lock. Children who touch it are "transferred." He turns mean, and biting is his favorite weapon.
4.  **Elena**, his first friend and first love, a child's love. They watch trains at sunset and talk about places with trees "too many to count." She gives him a useless small blue button with four holes and one chipped edge: "You don't keep things for for."
5.  **Tarik**, an older enemy over bread. In the uprising he kills a supervisor with a sorting tray ("Go\!") and dies holding off a poison zombie with a pipe.
6.  **The uprising, age 8**: zombies come up from the basement. Isaac and Elena hide under a bed on the top floor. Her ankle is seized. He holds her hand with both of his, and it slips out finger by finger. She screams his name once. He tries to stay silent with his hands over his mouth and fails by one small sound. The bed is lifted, and he sees her headless in the zombie's hands with fire behind it. A burning beam falls. He crawls out through glass and fire (the burn on his wrist), drops into an alley, calls her name and Tarik's, and no one comes out. He loses the button in the burning house.
7.  **The road, age 8–9**: theft, biting, being called rat and little savage. He eats grain, rotten fruit, boiled leather, beetles, bark, and finally dead headcrabs, crying before, after, or not at all; the not-crying frightens him. He won't go near cellars or basements. He runs from a woman offering soup because a child laughed inside. He falls through ice, survives a night under tree roots, and chips a tooth. Three wolves track him; he fights them on a tractor cab, later finds the leader dead and eats it, and doesn't cry. Design note: hollow and deadly like child Guts from Berserk, not a comic gremlin.
8.  **The Orchards, age 9**: eats fallen figs until he's sick and collapses. When he bites Samir (one of A'Had's sons), Samir doesn't hit him; he holds the back of his neck gently. A'Had, catching six figs under his shirt: "You work tomorrow." Com crouches at a distance every day; Isaac throws a rock, Com says "Good arm" and comes back. Isaac bites the cheek of a boy who imitates a zombie moan. He sleeps outside under the wall. In the other version, A'Had and Com leave honey bread, the first delicious thing he's ever tasted; he tries to leave, his body gives out at the boundary, and he never announces that he's staying.

### **4.3 Isaac and Aage, in order**

1.  **The wolf night** (LOCKED): the night after Aage's secret episode, a wolf comes for Isaac at the orchard's edge. Aage's arrow, then his knife, in killer mode. Isaac sees the blankness in Aage's eyes and knows something is wrong with him. No words.
2.  **The bread fight** (LOCKED, the next day): Ezra, two months old, asleep on Aage's chest. The fight is over bread meant for Ezra; Isaac doesn't know babies can't eat bread and mocks him. He bites the prosthetic hand through the glove (tooth marks) and swings a punch that arcs toward Ezra. Aage throws him and pins both wrists with the metal hand. Isaac's face goes rage, then fear, then shame at being seen afraid. He cries for real and screams "FUCK YOU\!", which startles Ezra and snaps Aage out of it. Aage lets go, sets the crushed bread on the table and leaves without turning around so Isaac can take it with dignity. Years later: "It was a metal hand." / "Still hurt my feelings." / "I was ten." / "A very accomplished little shit."
3.  **The frosty period**: constant arguments. Isaac sees Aage as terrifying and predatory but also as the thing that saved him. Part of him wants an unspoken "I know you"; anger is his only language for reaching.
4.  **The snowball fight** (LOCKED, Isaac 11, Ezra 1): Aage comes back brooding after turning Nadia down. Isaac attacks him for not helping set up Christmas, and it becomes a long, ugly shouting match with both of them screaming. Isaac stomps off. Aage lightly lobs a snowball at his back. Isaac spins and fires one into Aage's face with every ounce he has, then gives a mean little smile. Aage acts offended and vengeful, taking him seriously without escalating. It turns into real play, trees and walls as cover, talking shit, laughing. Isaac realizes he's happy, gets scared, his laugh breaks into crying, and he runs. Aage doesn't chase him or make it a therapy moment.
5.  **Christmas Eve**: Isaac stays away from Aage through the speeches and candle lighting. Then Aage feels a small bump against his shoulder and Isaac is just sitting there, arms folded, jaw tight, ears red, looking everywhere else. Aage almost smiles, doesn't, and lets him have the lie. Later his shoulder loosens against Aage's.
6.  **Silent attachment**: he appears beside Aage without asking: following, working nearby, hunting, riding, while denying all of it. Hadi is his rival, a "loud farm ox" to Isaac's "sneaking rat."
7.  **The crowbar** (age 13 or 14, OPEN): Isaac pulls a rotten fence board with a rusty crowbar from the shed, and it rings off the post. Aage's body seizes with no fear and no flashback. Isaac screams for Com, sobs "Don't you fucking die," and holds Aage's head in his lap as carefully as a bowl filled too full. "You stopped breathing." / "I'm throwing that fucking thing in the river." He doesn't ask what happened. Aage puts a shaking hand on his knee, and Isaac doesn't move it. Afterward: "Never fucking do that again." He won't understand what the crowbar did until he's grown.
8.  **The Depot** (LOCKED, Isaac 13, Ezra 3): Com needs a field surgical unit for Tomas and Petros, from a depot marked with three vertical bars and a circle. Isaac teaches Ezra to throw a wooden knife and gives him his own "for the horses." He sets a fallen grave cross upright. Inside, he moves with trained competence, with flashes of wonder at screens he quickly hides. Aage's handprint opens a checkpoint: "AUTHORITY RECOGNIZED." The medical wing is a morgue with child-sized shapes, and a small child's shoe breaks him. A corpse attacks; he freezes (zombies are his deepest wound), is slashed open, and turns: "Dad?" Aage carries him out through shamblers and runners repeating "Stay with me." On the ride home his defenses are stripped, and Aage finds it unbearable and would give anything to have the thorns back.
9.  **Surgery** (from Ezra's POV): the unit has to be struck to work. Com: "If this fails, he dies." Aage must hold him down: "Not almost. Not gently." Isaac: "No, Dad, please." Aage calls him "Son" to pull him back. "I'm cold" in a hot room; "I know," "I'm here." "Am I home?" "Yes." "Okay."
10. **Fever and the telling**: three nights of reaching for Elena, the bed, the button. Aage holds him and lets him bite a sleeve. Lucid, Isaac tells everything, from the train to the orchard. "I let go." "I had her hand." Aage doesn't argue him out of it: "I know what it is to live because someone else did not." He tells Isaac he carried her as far as a child could carry anyone. "I don't remember her voice right anymore… I remember the bad part best." / "It will still be there. But they stop being the only thing in the room." "Am I like them?" No, because he's ashamed and still knows the difference. "Don't leave." / "I won't." Aage's hand on the bed where Isaac can choose, and Isaac's fingers against his knuckles. By day four he's unbearable again, which is how they know he's healing. Ezra brings honey bread and Isaac gives half to Aage. On the wall: "I don't want to be like this forever." / "We can stop making our wounds into laws." He leans his shoulder against Aage, says he'll deny it, then at the door: "Dad?… Come on. It's cold."
11. **Age 14, Stay With Me**: with stitches in his side, he gets out of bed when Aage has a flashback nightmare and talks about horses, a broken chair and Ezra stealing bread until Aage comes back. Aage's speech is wrecked, and Isaac makes out "Love. Boys." Isaac: "You know I love you, right?" The first "I love you" between them; both are afraid of it.
12. **Age 14, The Thing That Stayed**: sits on the floor in pain through another flashback. "I just need you to stop hurting yourself. And I need you to know you're still my dad." They sleep with Isaac's hand in Aage's.
13. **After a reckless stunt**: "I'm not angry that you're alive."
14. **Ages 14–16**: a patrol route he treats like knighthood. An old bunker with children's bunks makes him go quiet, and Aage just sits with him. Aage makes him help feed a starving captured thief before judgment: justice without cruelty.
15. **Age 16, The Brush Devil**: a creature knocks Aage down. Isaac waits for a clear shot, fires both barrels, then grins. "Good work, son." / "I saved your life."
16. **Age 16, The Old Commander**: a drainage-pit creature grabs Isaac. Aage beats it far past death. Isaac takes his wrist: "It's dead." Aage says he's sorry; "You scared me." On the walk home his hand finds Aage's.
17. **Bunkhouse fire** (DRAFT, teen): "You went through a fucking wall… There was a door." / "The door was burning." / "So was the wall\!" / "I know." / "Stop saying that." He grabs Aage's shirt the way he used to grab food, knocks his fist on his chest like a door, cries into his sternum: "I didn't see you come out." He slaps out the fire on Aage's shoulder, bandages the bite with a wrong knot and redoes it twice: "This is stupid." / "Yes." / "You're stupid." / "Yes." He sits knee to knee with Aage until the fire burns down.
18. **Ages 16–18**: he outrides Aage. A stranger asks who Aage is, and Isaac says "My father" without thinking; Aage lets it stand. They're known as the Road Wardens. Matt, the quiet spared boy his own age, becomes an unlikely close friend; Isaac inexplicably softens with him.
19. **City 20 return** (18–20+): "I think I can go now." At the station: "I thought maybe she had gotten out after me. I knew she hadn't. But I kept looking." / "You were a child." / "I know," and this time it's true. At the basement door: "No one should have put children above that." In the burned top-floor room he kneels in the ash: "I know I couldn't have pulled you back. But I'm sorry I let go." He finds the blue button under burned metal, still blue, ash in its four holes. "She said you don't keep things for for." He buries it at the base of a young tree with a strip of Com's linen tied around it: "I made it to the trees." He also builds Elena a grave marker.
20. **Courtship and marriage**: Sarah, ginger-haired. They met at about 13 as friends and grew up together. Aage gives terrible courtship advice and Ezra gives better advice. A real moral fight with Aage matures the bond. They marry as adults, weeks before the finale.
21. **The Things We Carry** (about 25, winter): Aage finally tells Isaac his whole history. Isaac realizes what the crowbar did, cries and apologizes. "You gave me a life, Isaac. A real one." Snowballs on the walk home.
22. **Finale**: cleaning a rifle that doesn't need it, listening while pretending not to. Sent away, he knows Aage is sending him off to save him: "You're sending me away." He straps Aisha's girth "like he was trying to hurt the buckle." "I love you, Dad\!" He rides three miles; "Please. Not yet." His sarcasm burns away: "They're coming\!" With Hadi, A'Had's last son and his brother in all but name ("Then let's fucking go."), he leads forty to fifty riders in navy barding with shaped-charge spears out of a seam of white light. A round tears past his head and he doesn't turn it. One long sound with everything in it, and his spear goes under Aaron's collarbone and out the back; Aisha carries the body four strides. Aaron's blood runs down the shaft toward his gloves, the accidental contact through which Lucifer tries to take him and the Vortigaunts stop it. Aage dies with him there. Isaac and Ezra bury him beside Justine. In the epilogue Sarah says she wants children; Aage never meets them.

### **4.4 Psychology and voice**

  - **Core guilt**: "I let go." He knows he was a child; the guilt eases and is never erased.
  - **Defenses**: biting, swearing, stealing, refusing help, looking dangerous instead of afraid. Being seen afraid is the worst shame.
  - **Triggers**: zombies, headcrabs, cellars and basements, beds being lifted, slow things moving in the dark, a wet cough in the dark, a small child's shoe, cooking smoke, raised voices. His body freezes; that's physiology, not cowardice.
  - **Habits that last years**: hiding food, eating too fast, sleeping with his back to a wall, waking with both hands clamped over his mouth, reaching for a knife.
  - **What changes**: the biting, the food hiding, flinching from touch, refusing comfort, silence about love. By 14 he can say "I love you." By 16 he steps in to stop Aage. As a man he cries openly and talks about Elena.
  - **What never changes**: swearing, arguing, teasing ("old man," "stubborn old bastard"), pride, hiding fear behind a joke. He argues about small things (snare tightness, who rides better) as a way of staying close, says the true thing and then undercuts it ("If you tell anyone I did that, I'll deny it"), cries angrily and wipes his face on his sleeve, and when he's scared for Aage says it plainly ("I was scared for you, Dad"). He's protective of Ezra under the insults. At 13, frightened, he calls him "Aage," then "Dad."
  - **Voice rule**: fast profanity, hostility, sarcasm. Fear and humiliation make his speech shorter, not longer. His vulnerability is strongest when language fails him.

## **PART 5 — EZRA**

### **5.1 Vital facts**

  - Name whispered by his dying mother on a small mountain after a catastrophic birth. She is unnamed, his father unknown, and he has no surname. One of the first babies born after the suppression field fell: ordinary childhood itself becomes revolutionary after a decade without babies.
  - Aage is not his biological father. Aage becomes his everything: father, mother, shelter, warmth, food, weapon. "I will keep him safe." Aage thinks of him as "my baby."
  - Exactly ten years younger than Isaac. About 2 months at the wolf night and bread fight; 1 at the snowball fight; 3 at the Depot (default age 3 if a Depot-era scene doesn't say). His age at the finale is unstated: roughly a teenager or young man.
  - Look: as a newborn, the "fattest face" wrapped in a sweatshirt, crystal-blue eyes, looking up confused rather than scared. Then a chubby toddler with dark hair and big eyes. Small for his age: "I'm supposed to be short. I'm little."

### **5.2 Personality (LOCKED)**

  - Pure sunshine, "gooey." Never says anything negative, never insults, never complains nastily. Innocent, curious, literal, warm and generous. Includes everyone: birds, squirrels, mice, other children, adults.
  - Fascinated by Christmas lights, animals, ornaments, people, Miri, anything beautiful.
  - Afraid of storms. In the Thunderstorm scene he bursts into Aage's tent soaked and screaming, climbs onto him like a monkey, and cries all night. Isaac comes in "to see if Ezra got here okay" and ends up asleep against Aage too, rolling away when caught.
  - Mischief: steals bread from the kitchen and denies it with crumbs on his face. Has "a fox's hunting instincts when sweets were involved." Competitive and proud.
  - His arc is outward. He doesn't change; he changes others. Isaac's arc is inward and tangled.
  - WORKING: Ezra as the light, the conduit, a child who may see the dead. If he does, it costs him something quiet. He might notice, for example, that the dead look calm, and ask why.

### **5.3 Ezra's life, in order**

1.  **The finding**: Aage comes down the mountain carrying new life after years as an instrument of death.
2.  **The Embryo Stage**: a pitch-black, warm room smelling of sweat, old blankets, warm milk and baby breath. Aage and Ezra under blankets look from the doorway like one shapeless organism. Aage's body stops knowing where he ends and Ezra begins; if someone carries the baby across the room, Aage feels the distance physically. Ezra sleeps through Aage vomiting over the side of the bed and wakes only when Aage tries to put him down. "Out of every face in the world, he knew mine." Mara brings soup; Aage glares; "I will not take the baby." Later: "Keep your baby." The word "my" hits Aage hard. Someone leaves a carved wooden bird at the door; Aage throws it in the corner, then washes it and gives it to Ezra, who keeps dropping it on Aage's chest "as though the bird had betrayed him."
3.  **The community**: his milestones are public events because no one has seen a baby in ten years: first smile, first laugh, first steps, first scraped knee, first birthday. A'Had: "Listen… We're loud again."
4.  **The danger no one knows about** (LOCKED): the night Aage nearly hurt him in a rebound episode. Ezra never knows.
5.  **The bread fight**: asleep on Aage's chest through it until Isaac's "FUCK YOU\!"
6.  **Bath day** (about 4, domestic): Aage washes him in the orchard with his good hand while the metal hand rests on the basin rim. Ezra holds two of the metal fingers anyway. Isaac chops wood loudly outside, and Ezra reports on Hadi and the goat as if they were the weather.
7.  **Christmas Eve** (toddler): entranced by everything. Called forward with Miriam, Nadia's adopted daughter. Miri takes his hand, they walk beneath the candles while a little wooden angel turns above them, she whispers the first words, his face lights up as he remembers, and they sing a carol into the snowy night. Foreshadowing of the love they'll grow into.
8.  **The Depot** (3): Isaac's wooden knife "for the horses." He stays outside with the horses as a real job and calms them with his cheek against Justine's neck. He hauls the heavy surgical case onto Aisha alone and mounts alone. On the ride home he keeps shouting "Don't be quiet\!" and sobbing "I have it\!" He won't let go of the case even after a bad dismount. Com: "It matters. Bring it."
9.  **The surgery** (his POV): in the corner with the wooden knife in both hands. He can't see Isaac. He hears "If this fails, he dies" and knows it as fact. He drops the knife and covers his ears when Isaac screams. Mara's hand rests on his shoulder and doesn't pull him in. "Dad's back was the whole wall of the world now." Afterward he's forgotten in the corner for a while. Aage tells him some things hurt people for no reason. He is scared, but not poisoned.
10. **Feeling displaced**: after the Depot, all the attention goes to Isaac and Ezra briefly feels left out. Aage learns that fatherhood means holding both sons.
11. **Honey bread**: brings it to fevered Isaac. "Leave." "Short." / "I'm supposed to be short. I'm little." Isaac mutters thanks.
12. **The Fire Goes Out** (years later): asleep beside Aage, mostly grown out of needing to be pinned to him every night. Aage wakes with burning on his back. Ezra, without waking, reaches across the empty sheet and finds Aage's shirt, exactly as he did as a baby. What calms Aage: "Ezra had never been there. The men in that room had never touched him. Ezra had happened afterward."
13. **Matt**, the ordinary spared boy, sometimes sneaks him bread wrapped in a cloth from his mother's breakfast table, "for the little one… He's growing." Isaac: "He eats more than me." Nobody says why.
14. **Bunkhouse fire** (DRAFT): runs from the paddock, stops dead at the sight of Aage's burned back, and sits down on his other side without a word.
15. **Adventure ideas** (not committed): his lost doll, which Isaac secretly tracks down; holding the lantern; a bird's nest he insists on saving; his first fish while Isaac pretends not to care; a funeral for a rescued animal; a storm in an old stone chapel; asking whether the rabbits on the snare line have families.
16. **Miriam**: toward the end of the book, when both are grown, they begin to love each other. In the first Combine attack she's struck by a falling burning log. Aage brings her body to Tarsus. Ezra is destroyed. He sits beside her in the third row holding her hand through the blanket and doesn't cry. Elegy: "She gave him something the world had never given him before. The right to hope for what had not happened yet… That hope is not buried with her. It belongs to him now." Aage kneels and they lift her together.
17. **After Aage**: he and Isaac bury their father beside Justine.

### **5.4 What Ezra means**

  - Ezra reaches Aage below language and argument, at the level of the caregiver animal: warmth, smell, breathing, feeding, guarding. He teaches Aage's body that closeness can continue without punishment.
  - Ezra reaches the body first; Isaac reaches identity.
  - Through his life, adolescence and young adulthood, the gooeyness stays. He never hardens into another Isaac.

## **PART 7 — STYLE, VOICE AND MOTIFS**

### **7.1 Global rules**

  - Show, don't explain. Let action carry subtext; the reader discovers it.
  - Violence reveals character and consequence; it's never neutral "cool" texture. Aage's violence switches on abruptly.
  - Domestic life is not filler. Chores, meals, rides, repairs, animals, weather and children are how Act III proves change.
  - Humor comes from character friction and physical circumstance (Isaac's mouth, Aage's dryness, Ezra's literalness).
  - Spiritual material works first as pressure, image, ambiguity and behavior, and only rarely as explanation.
  - Prose: clean, short declaratives at moments of crisis, concrete sensory detail, few adjectives, no purple metaphors piled up. Sentence fragments at peaks are fine ("Sky." / "Then nothing.").
  - When Aage is central, write in his first person, past tense.

### **7.2 Scene patterns that work**

  - Conflict → physical action → silence → a tiny choice (especially Aage and Isaac).
  - Ordinary task → old-world reminder → reaction without explanation (roads and ruins).
  - Child's question → adult discomfort → simple answer → later thematic echo (Ezra).
  - Public ritual → private microgesture (Christmas speeches, then Isaac's shoulder bump).
  - Epic action → aftermath that recontextualizes it (Act II and the finale).
  - Loss → carrying an obligation rather than nihilism.

### **7.3 Motifs**

  - "Come with me": Justine's phrase, hijacked by Breen, answered at the end. Never cheapen it with overuse.
  - The big blue sky: peace, breadth, something that asks nothing and can't be owned. Aage after captivity: what he liked was that "nothing happened."
  - Hands and carrying: who holds whom. Bread left on a table. A hand on a bed, offered to be chosen. Carrying Isaac out of the Depot; carrying the dead.
  - Bread and honey bread: hunger, trust, gift.
  - The white lion: the child-self, reintegration. A lion sleeps behind a hill at the end.
  - The blue button: a useless thing kept because it's useless. Love.
  - Crowbars, basements and stairs: the body's memory.
  - Noise returning: "We're loud again."
  - Flowers in skulls, vines over ruins, seeds beneath ash.
  - Horses and roads; snow and blue-hour light for the thaw; navy barding.
  - "Someone has to be the last one."

## **PART 8 — HOW TO WRITE A WARRIOR CHAPTER**

1.  **Place it.** Act, rough chapter band or arc, season, and the ages of Aage, Isaac and Ezra (use Part 4.1 and 5.1). Check the order: wolf night, bread fight, frosty period, snowball fight and Christmas Eve, silent attachment, crowbar, Depot, surgery and fever, recovery scenes, ages 14–16, ages 16–18, City 20, courtship and marriage, finale.
2.  **Pick a POV**: Aage first person (default), or close third on Isaac or Ezra when the drafts do (the surgery is Ezra's POV; the finale ride is Isaac in third person).
3.  **Choose one emotional movement**, not five. Example: Isaac admits fear without saying it.
4.  **Anchor it in a physical task**: fixing a fence, a snare line, a horse lesson, a well, a hunt, the bread, an injury.
5.  **Keep the damage present**: Aage watches doors, sits facing exits, flexes the glove, is careful with the crowbar. Isaac hides food, sleeps against a wall, rubs his wrist scar. Ezra is fearless with affection.
6.  **End on a small, unexplained gesture** rather than a speech: a shoulder against a shoulder, half the bread handed back, a hand left where it could be taken.
7.  **Good open territory** (beauty-years material the author wants): Isaac's first hunt with Aage; the snare line and Ezra asking whether rabbits have families; Ezra's lost doll, which Isaac secretly tracks down; the broken well; a horse lesson; a night patrol; a bandit sign on the road; exploring ruins; a missing child; a bandit boy; the stone-chapel storm; Isaac and Hadi's rivalry; Isaac and Matt's odd friendship; Ezra and Miri as children; the Road Wardens; Aage's terrible courtship advice; the moral fight between Aage and Isaac.
8.  **Re-check Section A and Part 1 before you finish.**
