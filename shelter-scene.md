# Shelter — A Romantic Scene

The shelter is nothing fancy. A concrete floor, a few mattresses, walls that don't quite keep out the cold. Aage sits in the corner, the place he always chooses—back against the wall, eyes on the door. It's the only way he can breathe in a space like this.

He doesn't look up when Isaac comes in, though he knows immediately. He's learned to recognize the sound of Isaac's footsteps the way some people recognize a voice.

Isaac crosses the room and sits down beside him. Not across from him. Beside. Close enough that their shoulders almost touch. This is what Isaac does now—he chooses proximity over distance, every time, as though the space between them is a wound that won't stop bleeding if he doesn't keep it closed.

"You're here," Isaac says. It's not a question. His voice carries the specific relief of someone who's been afraid all day that the answer might be no.

"I'm here," Aage confirms, and something in his chest loosens. This is the antidote now. This phrase. This person. The fact that Isaac came and found him in this shitty shelter instead of disappearing like everyone else has.

They sit in silence. Aage can feel the tremor in Isaac's body—the aftereffects of hypervigilance, the way a nervous system learns to stay braced for disaster. He recognizes it because his own body does the same thing. Two damaged instruments learning to play in the same key.

After a long time, Isaac's hand finds Aage's. Not tentatively. With certainty. Like he knows he has the right to reach for this, to need this. It's taken years for him to believe that. Aage watches the movement—Isaac's fingers sliding between his own, gripping like Isaac is drowning and Aage is the only solid thing in the world.

"I had a bad day," Isaac says quietly. His fingers tighten. "I kept thinking something would take you away."

Aage doesn't offer false reassurance. They both know better than that. The world is full of things that take people away. But he turns his hand over and squeezes back, presses his palm flat against Isaac's.

"I'm still here," he says instead.

It's not a promise that nothing bad will happen. It's a promise that right now, in this moment, they are both still alive. They are both still choosing to show up. They are both still trying, even though trying is exhausting for people as broken as they are.

Isaac leans his head against Aage's shoulder. His breathing slowly begins to even out. Aage can feel it—the panic subsiding, replaced by something that isn't quite peace but is close enough to peace that it might save his life.

"I love you," Isaac says. The words come out small and uncertain, like he's still not sure he's allowed to say them out loud.

Aage presses his cheek against the top of Isaac's head. "I know," he says. "I love you too."

It's not romantic in the way stories usually mean. There's no music. There's no candlelight. There's a concrete floor and the sound of wind outside and two people who've both been broken in ways that never fully heal, learning to exist in the same space without destroying each other.

But it's the most romantic thing either of them has ever experienced: being known. Being chosen anyway. Being held by someone who understands exactly how dark it gets inside your head and doesn't run away.

Isaac's breathing becomes slower, deeper. The tremor in his body begins to fade. After a while, he's asleep against Aage's shoulder, his grip on Aage's hand never loosening even in unconsciousness.

Aage sits there in the dark, in the shelter, with this broken boy asleep against him. He doesn't sleep. He just keeps watch, the way he always does. Because this—Isaac sleeping safely, Isaac believing he is safe, Isaac trusting that Aage will still be here in the morning—this is the only redemption Aage has ever found.

This is enough. For now, this is everything.
