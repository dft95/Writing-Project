# Chapter 2: The Pond

I was reading in my chair when I heard it.

The floorboard in the hallway—the third one from the bedroom door. The one that always creaked if you stepped on it. Isaac knew it creaked. He'd learned years ago which boards to avoid if he wanted to move through the house unseen.

He stepped on it deliberately.

I didn't move. I kept my eyes on the page in front of me, though I hadn't been reading, not really. The words were still sliding past without landing. But I heard him. I heard the message in that creak—not an accident, but an invitation. A signal in the language only he and I shared. The language of sound and movement and knowing without words.

He was going to the pond.

I let him move past. Let him reach the door, let him open it, let him step out into the dark. I could hear these things—not with my ears exactly, but with something deeper. The settling of the house as it registered his leaving.

Then I set the book down.

The night air was cool when I stepped outside. There was a lamp hung by the door—oil, old, the kind that had survived everything. I took it down and lit it, the flame catching and steadying. The light pushed back the dark about six feet in all directions, and beyond that was nothing but trees.

I knew the path. I'd walked it enough times—following Isaac on nights like this. Letting him get ahead until he thought he was truly alone. Then appearing.

The path went through the trees and down toward the water. I could hear it before I could see it—the small sounds of a pond at night, the shift of things moving in it, the gentle lap of water.

Then I saw him.

He was at the edge of the tree line, a figure in the darkness, turning back toward where he knew I'd be coming from. For a moment we were both still—him knowing I had come, me knowing that he had wanted me to. The lamp light didn't reach him, but I could make out his shape, the way he was standing.

He turned away from me, toward the water, and began walking.

I stayed where I was, giving him that space. That mercy. He needed to reach the water first. He needed those moments alone with it before I joined him.

He walked to the edge of the pond. The water was black, still, reflecting nothing. For a moment he stood there, silhouetted against the dark. Then he began undressing—pulling his shirt over his head, pushing down his pants. I could see the pale line of his skin as he undid buttons, as he stepped out of cloth and stood bare in the night air.

Then he walked into the water.

He didn't hesitate. He went in like he was answering a call, like the water had been waiting for him specifically. The cold would take his breath. I knew that. But he needed that—the shock of something real, something that couldn't pretend to be gentle.

He waded deeper, the water rising up his legs, his waist, his chest. His movements had the quality of someone both desperate and grateful, like the water itself was answering a question he'd been asking.

I set the lamp down on the rocks and began to undress.
