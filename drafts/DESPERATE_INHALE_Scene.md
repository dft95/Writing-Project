# A Desperate Inhale — First Recognition

**Timeline: Orchards, Isaac 13, Aage adult. The day after the bread fight.**

---

Isaac found him at the well before sunrise.

Not looking for him. Just standing there, hands gripping the stone rim like it might collapse, staring into the dark water. The rope hung slack. No one had drawn from it yet. The settlement was still asleep—or trying to be.

Isaac knew the particular quality of Aage's stillness. It was different from sleep. It was the stillness of someone listening to their own body the way you'd listen for footsteps in a cellar.

He should have left. Should have gone to the fig trees where he belonged, where nobody could touch him. But he didn't move.

"Your hand okay?" Isaac's voice came out rough. Unused.

Aage didn't turn. His prosthetic hand, still gloved from yesterday, lay open on the stone. The glove had a crease where Isaac's teeth had marked it. The other hand—the real one—was shaking.

"Shouldn't have done that," Isaac said. He meant *to* Aage. He meant *at all*. The words came out wrong.

"No," Aage said quietly. "You shouldn't have."

He still didn't turn around.

Isaac felt something seize in his chest. The familiar thing: the moment before. The moment before pain. Before running. Before—

Aage turned his head. Just his head. His eyes found Isaac's in the pre-dawn gray, and Isaac saw it immediately: the other man recognized something too. Not recognition the way people knew faces. Recognition the way a drowning man recognizes the surface.

Aage's left hand was still shaking. Isaac could see it now. Could see the tremor work through the forearm, up into the shoulder, until Aage pressed it flat against his ribs to still it.

"You're afraid of me," Aage said. It wasn't a question.

Isaac's jaw tightened. "No."

"You are." Aage breathed in, slow. His whole body moved with it—breath dropping into him like weight into water. "You should be. I nearly—" He stopped. "Yesterday. When you swung at him. I nearly broke your wrist."

"You didn't."

"I could have. I wanted to." Aage's voice was strange. Flat and hot at once. "Part of me wanted to very badly. Do you understand?"

Isaac understood. He understood perfectly. He'd wanted to hurt too, wanted to bite and claw and make something bleed that wasn't himself. That was the only language he knew. That was what it meant to touch anything.

"I'm not—" Aage stopped. Tried again. "I don't hurt children."

Isaac felt something in him crack open like rotten fruit. "I'm not a child."

"You are."

The word hung between them. Isaac wanted to run. Wanted to bite. Wanted to scream that Aage didn't know, didn't *know* what he'd done, what he'd survived, what he'd had to become—

Aage stepped backward. Away from the well. Away from Isaac.

He raised his real hand, slow enough that Isaac could have struck him, and held it open. Palm forward. Empty.

"I'm going to sit down," Aage said. "Right here. I'm not going anywhere. If you want to leave, leave. If you want to stay, you can stay."

He sat. Right there on the stones. His back against the well wall. His breathing still strange—like he was learning it for the first time.

Isaac didn't move. Couldn't. It was like being frozen in the apartment again, pressed under the bed while everything burned. Except this time—

This time nothing was burning.

Aage's left hand had stopped shaking. It rested on his knee. Open. Useless. Defenseless.

"I understand," Aage said, to the sky or to himself or to Isaac, it didn't matter. "About wanting to hurt. About thinking the only way to survive is to bite."

Isaac's breath came short. Fast. Like he was drowning standing up.

"I know what it is," Aage continued, "to live so long thinking the world is only teeth. To think you have to have teeth bigger than everyone else or you'll be eaten." He paused. "I know what it is when you meet someone and you think, for one second, that maybe—"

He didn't finish.

Isaac took one step toward him. Then another. His legs felt like they belonged to someone else, like they were making decisions his mind hadn't approved.

He sat down. Not close. Three feet away. Far enough that Aage couldn't grab him. Far enough that Isaac could run if he needed to.

They sat in silence while the sky started to change color. While birds started to wake in the orchard. While the settlement began to stir behind them.

"I won't hurt him again," Isaac said. His voice was so quiet it barely carried.

Aage closed his eyes. "I know."

"I didn't mean to. I just—when I saw—"

"I know."

They sat with that. With the space between them. With the terrible, fragile thing of being understood.

Isaac felt his ribs expand. Felt his lungs pull in a breath that wasn't shallow or guarded. Felt, for the first time since the fire, like there might be air enough for both of them. Like the world was wide enough that two people could fit in it without one of them disappearing.

It terrified him.

"You can't leave," he said. It came out like a threat, but it wasn't. It was a plea.

Aage opened his eyes. Looked at him. Looked at him *really*, the way nobody had ever looked at Isaac—not as prey, not as a threat, not as a thing to be managed. Just as a person. As someone who mattered enough to look at.

"I won't," Aage said.

It was a promise. It sounded impossible. It sounded like a lie. But Aage's hand was still open, still empty, still on his knee where Isaac could see it.

The sun broke over the trees. Light fell across the well, across the two of them sitting in it. Isaac felt it on his face like a hand he wasn't afraid of. Like something gentle and terrible and necessary all at once.

He breathed. 

And breathed.

And didn't run.
