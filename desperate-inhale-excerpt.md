# A Desperate Inhale — Excerpt

The mattress shifts under their weight, a sound swallowed by the dark. Isaac moves like he's trying to memorize the shape of this moment, like it's water he can hold if he just squeezes hard enough. His hands brace against Aage's chest, feeling the heartbeat beneath—proof of a living thing. Proof that he's still here.

Aage's breath comes broken, rough at the edges. His hands grip Isaac's waist, anchoring him, as though Isaac might dissolve if he lets go. This is the rhythm they've fallen into: desperate reaching, frantic reassurance, the violent insistence that *now, right now* is enough to prove forever. Tomorrow doesn't exist. Only the relief of being wanted, being held, being *filled*.

Isaac's forehead touches Aage's shoulder. His movements grow urgent, slipping into something that looks like panic but feels like safety. This is what they do when the quiet gets too loud, when the fear of losing each other rises faster than they can breathe it back down.

Then the door slams open.

Isaac jolts so hard his knee cracks against the wall. He's moving before his brain catches up with the sound—scrambling backward, pulling away, his body responding to the intrusion with the speed of something hunted. Aage's hands slip from his waist as Isaac tumbles backward off the bed in a tangle of limbs that would be undignified if it weren't pure animal instinct.

"Aage—" Ezra's voice from the doorway, three years old and carrying the particular authority of a child who's learned that his voice is the only thing that stops the worst things from happening.

"I'm here," Aage says immediately, sitting up. His voice shifts—steadies itself into the shape of a protector. "Right here, Ezra. What is it?"

Isaac is already behind him, still shaking, pressing his back against the wall where the shadows are deepest. His breath comes fast and uneven. The need to touch Aage hasn't stopped; it's just been reclassified as forbidden, and that makes it worse.

Aage reaches back without looking, his hand finding Isaac's calf, running up to his knee—a small point of contact that says *I'm still yours even when I have to look at him.* His shoulders square toward Ezra, his whole body language shifting to: *I am here and there is nothing to fear.*

"Did you have a dream?" Aage asks, and the gentleness in his voice is so complete it erases everything else. 

Isaac listens to him talking to Ezra, watching the back of his head, his hand still anchored on Isaac's leg. The reassurance tastes like abandonment. The safety tastes like loss. This is the precision of their hunger: they fit together in ways that are illegible to anyone standing outside of it. And now there is an outsider. Now Isaac is hidden.

But Aage's hand doesn't move.

The air is not simple. It never is. But in this small point of contact—Aage's palm on Isaac's skin, Aage's attention on Ezra, Aage's body a wall between the child and what was happening just moments before—there is something that almost resembles a promise:

*I haven't forgotten you. Even now. Especially now.*

Isaac breathes. The world does not end.
