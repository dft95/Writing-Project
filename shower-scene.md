# The Shower

The water is warm. Aage stands under it with his eyes closed, letting it run over his scarred shoulders, his broken hands. For a few minutes, he can almost believe he's just a person washing dirt away, not someone whose mind is a graveyard of his own torture.

He doesn't hear Isaac come in over the sound of the water. He only knows because he feels the small body slip into the shower behind him, ducking under the spray without asking permission. This is what Isaac does now—he moves through the world as though proximity to Aage is survival.

"Hey," Aage says softly, turning to face him.

Isaac is small under the water, his hair plastered to his head. Twelve years old, but his eyes are ancient. He looks up at Aage with the specific hunger of someone who's never learned that safety can be real.

"I was scared," Isaac says. It's his constant refrain now. The fear lives in him like a second heartbeat.

"I'm here," Aage says. It's what he always says. It's the only thing that works.

Isaac steps closer, into Aage's space the way he always does. His small hands press against Aage's chest. Aage can feel his heart racing underneath his palms—that nervous system still convinced that every moment is borrowed, that disaster is coming.

Aage reaches up and washes Isaac's hair slowly, carefully. His damaged hands are gentle. Water runs down Isaac's face. His eyes stay fixed on Aage's, never looking away, as though Aage might disappear if he blinks.

"You're safe," Aage says, and both of them know it's a lie. Safety is temporary. Everything is temporary. But Aage says it anyway because Isaac needs to hear it, and Aage needs to say it, and for a moment under the warm water they can both pretend it's true.

Isaac's small body trembles against his. Not from cold. From the weight of staying alive in a world that tried to kill him. From the desperate, consuming need to be held by someone who understands exactly how dark it gets inside a head like his.

"Don't let me go," Isaac whispers.

"I won't," Aage says, and he pulls Isaac closer under the spray. "I've got you."

They stand there in the shower, water pouring over them, and for a moment the warmth and the wetness and the closeness is enough to quiet the screaming inside their heads. Just barely. Just enough to breathe.

This is what love looks like for people as broken as they are: standing under warm water, holding each other up, pretending that this moment might last.
